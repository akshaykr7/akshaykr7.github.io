Ph.D. work: vibration of radial tyres

My dissertation is titled Experimental and Waveguide-Based Finite Element Investigations for Analyzing Vibrations in Tyres with Anisotropy, Hyperelasticity, Viscoelasticity, and Geometric Nonlinearity, and it was supervised by Prof. Nachiketa Tiwari.

A tyre is a ring-shaped structure whose shape repeats all the way around its circumference, so it can be studied as a waveguide. We mesh only one cross-section of the tyre and then find how waves travel along the circumferential direction. This is much lighter than building a full three-dimensional model of the whole tyre.

I implemented the Waveguide Finite Element Method (WFEM) in MATLAB to model broadband vibration in radial tyres. The model includes the real material behaviour of rubber, which is hyperelastic, viscoelastic and anisotropic. I checked its predictions against ABAQUS simulations and against experimental modal analysis.

Current work

As a FARE Fellow (postdoctoral research) at IIT Kanpur, I am extending the waveguide finite element framework to include air-cavity and tyre-rotation effects. I am also working on a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) for a vibration problem.

Experimental methods

I use Experimental Modal Analysis with accelerometers and an impact hammer, and Digital Image Correlation with high-speed cameras, to measure how structures vibrate and to validate my models.
